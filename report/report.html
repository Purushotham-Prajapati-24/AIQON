CASCADE: stage-aware shipment ETA, delay propagation and explanation

Aiqon AI Internship take-home · model v1.3.0 · seed 42 · out-of-time test (Q4 peak) · 6/6 quality gates pass · generated 2026-10-13

Answer first. On a held-out, later-in-time test season, CASCADE's ETA error (MAE of the P50 final delay) is 24.2 h, which is 37% better than carry-forward (95% CI 25%–49%) and 31% better than a lane-level propagation prior. Its P10–P90 ranges cover 82% of outcomes (target 80%), P(late > 24 h) has Brier 0.179 and PR-AUC 0.87, and every P50 is shown as realised delay plus a per-stage projection of the delay still to come. Because a structural causal simulator records a cause ledger for every hour of delay, we can score that explanation: the stage projected to add the most future delay is the ledger's top future stage 42% of the time (top-2: 69%). Caveat: the point forecast and these stage explanations come from a mechanistic operator-rulebook projection; the learned median correction did not beat it out-of-time, so validation set its weight to 0.0. The ML contributes the P10–P90 spread, P(late) and the stage forecasts.

1 · Problem and definitions

A container shipment moves through a fixed stage graph: gate-in → origin departure → (hub arrival → hub departure) → destination arrival → discharge → customs release → gate-out → delivery. Direct lanes skip the hub. Each milestone ek has a planned and an actual time. All quantities are in hours, and positive means late.

delay(ek)= actual(ek) − planned(ek) Δk= delay(ek) − delay(ek−1): the stage increment (positive adds delay, negative absorbs it) D= delay(DELIVERED) = Σk Δk: total delay; the model predicts the remaining part R(t) = D − delayobs(t) ETAq(t)= planned delivery + observed delay so far + predicted remaining delay R̂q(t), for q ∈ {P10, P50, P90} late= 1[D > 24 h]; P(late) is reported with every ETA

Predictions are as-of nowcasts: at each of 9 checkpoints (booked … gate-out) the model sees only what was known at that instant, including forecasts (not realised values) of future congestion and weather.

2 · Data and ground truth

Instead of adding random noise to timestamps, we built a structural causal model (SCM) of 300 shipments on lanes between real Asian origins, hubs (Singapore, Colombo, Jebel Ali, Tanjung Pelepas) and European / US destinations. Shipments share voyages, so errors are correlated. Each mechanism below writes signed hours into a cause ledger (shipment, stage, cause, hours). Within a stage the ledger sums exactly to Δk, and over all stages to D. The true root cause is the stage with the largest positive Δk. Late rate in this run: 38%.

Table 1 · Logistics mechanics in the simulator (each produces ledger entries)
StageReal-world behaviour modelled
OriginCarrier reliability (latent), origin congestion and storms delay departure (shared by the voyage).
OceanWeather slows the vessel; the vessel recovers part of an incoming delay by speeding up (negative Δ, capped).
TransshipmentHub handling (longer when congested) must finish before the connection cut-off. A missed connection waits for the next sailing (weekly or twice-weekly headway), which is the main cascade amplifier.
Destination portKingman-like berth queue W ∝ ρ/(1−ρ); a vessel arriving late loses its berth window and waits longer (congestion × lateness interaction).
CustomsInspection probability rises with commodity risk and incomplete documents; release is shifted to the next business slot (weekends, local holidays).
InlandRail leaves at fixed times, so a missed cut-off waits for the next train; trucks need appointments; consignee receiving windows.

External factors. Daily port congestion ρ(t) (base + seasonality incl. the Q4 peak and the Lunar New Year backlog, plus AR(1) noise) and regional weather with storm episodes and the monsoon. For future dates the model only sees noisy as-of forecasts whose error grows with the horizon. Missingness is typed: structural (no hub on direct lanes → sentinel, never NaN); MCAR reporting gaps (rate 6%); MNAR gaps from poor carrier EDI feeds (up to 25%) and congested terminals that fail to report discharge. A report reaches the system after a carrier-dependent latency (median 0.5–6 h by feed quality), and delivery is always observed. Split: out-of-time. The last voyages by departure, which fall in the Q4 peak, form the test set (62 shipments, 725 as-of snapshots), so the test season is shifted relative to training. Assumptions: fixed schedules and headways per lane; costs from a configurable tariff; no strikes or blank sailings; one container per shipment.

3 · Approach

  • As-of checkpoints. Each shipment expands into one row per milestone. Features use only milestone reports that had arrived by the as-of time (an event that happened but was reported later is invisible), and leakage is property-tested.
  • Operator rulebook vs truth. A physics-style projection of every remaining stage (cut-offs, headways, Kingman queue, customs calendars, rail timetables) from forecast congestion and weather, with coefficients deliberately different from the simulator's, so the model cannot "know the simulator".
  • Propagation features. These include projected hub buffer versus cut-off, congestion × current lateness, days to the next business slot, rail cut-off slack, and missingness flags that carry signal (EDI tier, gaps so far).
  • Residual quantile GBM with temporal shrinkage. LightGBM models P10/P50/P90 of the residual on top of the rulebook (300 trees, 15 leaves). The learned median correction is blended in with weight λ ∈ {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, chosen on the latest training voyages and out-of-fold. λ = 0.0 was selected, so the median comes from the rulebook, and ML contributes uncertainty, P(late) and stage forecasts; the band uses the learned quantiles directly.
  • Drift-aware CQR and P(late). Conformalised quantile regression per checkpoint group (offset = max(out-of-fold, temporal-slice)) protects coverage under the seasonal shift. P(late) stacks the empirical residual distribution around the rulebook with the monotone classifier (logistic, fit out-of-fold).
  • Explanations. Stage bar = rulebook projected increment + λ × exact TreeSHAP of the residual model (pred_contrib); at λ = 0 the bars, and the attribution score, are the rulebook alone. Realised delay + stage bars + a floor/ordering adjustment = P50; templates write ops/customer/analyst narratives. Future-stage attribution is inherently noisy, so we report top-2 and lift, not just top-1.
  • Decision layer. Promise ETA with hysteresis, alerts, cost-of-delay, an intervention advisor (model re-run under a fixed action menu), a scenario radar and a prediction ledger; FastAPI with a fallback chain (lane propagation prior → carry-forward) and a Streamlit control tower.

5 · Limitations and next steps

Synthetic mechanisms, not fitted to real data (revisions and feed corrections are simulated); associational counterfactuals (advisor Spearman 0.45 vs true interventions); small n: 62 test shipments in 20 voyages, so CIs are wide. Alert timing: the LATE threshold (P(late) ≥ 0.7, actionable F1 on training folds) warns 61% of late test shipments ≥ 48 h ahead (71% ± 14% over seeds). Next: an AIS/EDI adapter, survival models, drift-triggered recalibration.
