CASCADE: predicting shipment ETA and explaining where delays come from

Aiqon AI Internship take-home · model v1.2.0 · 2026-10-13

Summary. CASCADE predicts when a container shipment will actually be delivered, how likely it is to be more than 24 hours late, and which stage of the journey is causing the delay. On a test season it never saw in training, its delivery-time error is 24.5 h, which is 37% lower than assuming today's delay stays the same. Because our simulated data records the true cause of every hour of delay, we could check the explanations too: the real cause is in CASCADE's top two stages 68% of the time.

1 · How ETA and delay are defined

Every shipment passes through the same stages: gate-in, origin departure, hub arrival and departure (only on hub lanes), destination arrival, discharge, customs release, gate-out and delivery. Each stage has a planned time and an actual time. All values are in hours, and a positive number means late.

Stage delayactual time − planned time at that stage Stage incrementhow much delay a stage adds (or recovers) compared with the stage before it Final delaydelay at delivery, which equals the sum of all stage increments ETAplanned delivery + delay so far + predicted remaining delay, given as P10, P50 and P90 Latefinal delay greater than 24 hours

At each milestone the model only sees what was known at that moment. Congestion and weather for future stages are given as forecasts, not as the values that actually happened.

2 · Data

We generated 300 shipments with a simulator built on real logistics rules, on lanes from Asian ports through hubs such as Singapore and Colombo to Europe and the US. Each rule in Table 2 writes the hours of delay it causes into a cause ledger, which gives us the true reason for every delay. Some milestone reports are deliberately missing or arrive late, as they do with real carrier data. The last voyages in time, which fall in the busy Q4 peak, are held out as the test set.

Table 2 · Real-world behaviour built into the simulator
StageWhat happens
OriginCarrier reliability, port congestion and storms delay departure.
OceanBad weather slows the vessel; ships speed up to recover part of a delay.
TransshipmentIf the container misses its connection, it waits for the next sailing. This is the biggest source of cascading delay.
Destination portA late vessel loses its berth slot and queues longer when the port is busy.
CustomsInspections depend on cargo risk and paperwork; release only happens in office hours.
InlandTrains leave at fixed times, trucks need appointments and receivers have delivery windows.

3 · Method

Table 3 · How CASCADE builds a prediction
StepWhat it does
SnapshotsOne row per shipment per milestone, using only information available at that time.
Operator rulebookProjects the delay each remaining stage will add, using cut-offs, sailing schedules, queues and calendars. Its settings are deliberately different from the simulator's.
Stage featuresLink stages together: hub buffer vs connection cut-off, congestion × current lateness, time to the next customs slot.
Machine learningLightGBM quantile models give the P10–P90 range, a calibrated classifier gives P(late), and conformal calibration keeps the range reliable when the season changes.
ExplanationThe prediction is split into delay already realised plus each remaining stage's share, and written as a short sentence for the customer or the planner.

On validation data, the learned correction to the median did not beat the rulebook, so it was switched off (weight 0.0). The median ETA therefore comes from the rulebook, and machine learning adds the range, the chance of being late and the stage forecasts.

Worked example: SHP-000174, Shanghai → Tanjung Pelepas → Rotterdam

SHP-000174 is expected 7.0 days late (likely 4.3 days–10.4 days late); 83% chance of more than 24 h late. Already 46 h behind plan. Main driver: Transshipment — projected to reach Tanjung Pelepas after the connection cut-off (buffer short by 36 h) (+126 h). Also contributing: Customs — customs release projected to fall outside office hours / on a non-working day at Rotterdam (+12 h). Suggested action: Check the next sailing from the hub and consider re-booking or priority loading. Confidence is reduced: 2 milestone(s) missing from the feed.

The biggest driver is a projected missed connection at the hub (126 h). The simulator's cause ledger confirms the true cause: Transshipment. Expected cost of the delay: $778.
